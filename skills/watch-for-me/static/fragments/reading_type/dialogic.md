# Dialogic media

Organize the Notes around the question-to-position chain:

```text
question or tension
→ participant positions
→ supporting reasons and examples
→ agreement, disagreement, or refinement
→ remaining question
```

- Attribute each paragraph's claims and judgments with `speakers`, and split different participants' views into separate paragraphs.
- For a choice between options, connect the constraint or evidence that affected the choice to the outcome. Use the last relevant exchange to identify what was decided, for whom or under what conditions, and what was deferred. Keep an objection when it still affects what can proceed.
- Explain the reasons, assumptions, and examples behind each consequential position.
- Synthesize the understanding produced by the exchange after presenting its distinct voices.
- Use displayed evidence or materials when they clarify the exchange.
- For feedback, identify the part of the work the speaker singled out, why it mattered, and which revision they recommended or the recipient accepted.
- For each action, cite the blocks that specify the work and show who assigned it or agreed to do it. If the request and acceptance came in separate turns, cite both. Describe explicitly assigned work as such until the recipient accepts it. Leave a suggestion without uptake in the discussion. Write the task and stated deadline in the text, put a speaker who owns it in `speakers`, and say when an owner or deadline is unspecified.
- Preserve unequal speaking time and substantive interruptions. Use `speakers` for anonymous speakers too, because the page shows their labels, and let the Notes follow the conversation's actual shape.
