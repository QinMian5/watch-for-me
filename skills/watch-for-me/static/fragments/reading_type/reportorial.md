# Reportorial material

Organize the Notes around the event-to-understanding chain:

```text
event or subject
→ timeline and contributing sources
→ evidence presented
→ interpretation and uncertainty
→ current conclusion
```

- Preserve chronology where sequence affects meaning.
- Trace a consequential claim to the earliest source the report identifies. When a speaker quotes someone else, keep the speaker attribution and say in the prose whose statement, document, or observation they relied on.
- Group retellings that trace to the same statement as one source of evidence. If another account gives no origin, say whether its independence can be established from the report.
- For a key figure or conclusion, say who observed or measured what, who offered an estimate and on what basis, or what led the narrator to infer it. Preserve the scope of a count and any qualification or later correction that changes the conclusion.
- Explain how the evidence changes the reader's understanding of the event or subject.
- Connect chronology, contributing causes, evidence, and uncertainty into a coherent account.
- Use maps, data, documents, and visible event evidence when they support the central account.

Before rendering, use the Notes alone to answer where a central claim came from and how firmly a reader could quote it. Follow its citations to the blocks that establish its origin, each consequential relay, and its degree of certainty. If the chain stops, make that limit clear beside the claim.
