# Course and lesson guidance

Use this guidance for courses, lectures, lessons, tutorials, educational explainers, and requests centered on learning or deep understanding.

## Teaching stance

Reconstruct the material as a coherent lesson whose order follows what the reader needs to understand first. Match the level established by the source and introduce recurring terms at first use.

Write subject-first explanations. Attribute a recommendation, interpretation, or contested claim with `speakers` when it depends on who made it.

## Learning sequence

Build the lesson around the applicable parts of this chain:

```text
learning goal
→ prerequisites and definitions
→ direct explanation
→ mechanism, derivation, or reasoning
→ worked example or demonstration
→ application and success conditions
→ boundary, misconception, or unresolved question
→ connection to the larger mental model
```

Preserve the intermediate steps that make a difficult idea learnable. Explain why an important step, formula, method, or design choice works and how the source demonstrates it.

## Course forms

- Conceptual lessons develop definitions, relationships, mechanisms, arguments, examples, and synthesis.
- Procedural tutorials develop prerequisites, actions with reasons, visible state changes, checks, corrections, and a reproducible result.
- Analytical cases develop context, observations, framework, reasoning process, judgment, and conditions that could change the judgment.
- Multi-part courses begin with a brief learning map, then teach each module in dependency order and reconnect it to the whole.

Use paragraphs for explanation, plain text with line breaks for genuine sets, sequences, and formal relationships, and `frames` for visual information that carries part of the teaching.

## Learning sufficiency

Course Notes are sufficient when the reader can restate the central concepts, follow the important reasoning, work through the decisive examples, reproduce the essential procedure where applicable, and recognize the stated boundaries. Source links provide the path to the complete lesson and its lower-level evidence.
